# Maya Chen UGC Content Goal

## Working Project

Maya is building a short UGC-style content package for her creator business:

**Project name:** `Tiny Apartment Reset Kit`

**Series promise:** practical five-minute reset routines for busy renters who want their apartment, meals, and body to feel manageable without needing a perfect lifestyle.

**Audience:** renters in their 20s and 30s who are tired after work, overwhelmed by small spaces, and attracted to realistic calm content that feels doable.

**Business purpose:** promote Maya's $27 meal-planning Notion template and grow her email list with a free "5-minute reset checklist."

## Content Package Maya Is Trying To Build

Maya's near-term goal is to create a small set of reusable assets:

1. One vertical cover image for a Reel or TikTok about a five-minute after-work apartment reset.
2. One product-style visual for her meal-planning template.
3. One calm lifestyle image she can use as B-roll inspiration or a carousel background.
4. One saved prompt she can reuse next week.
5. One project or library location where she can find these assets again.

The package does not need to be perfect. Maya wants something usable enough to publish, test, and iterate.

## Maya's Realistic Work Session Context

Maya is usually testing ShortPulse during a narrow work window between client tasks. She may be tired, mildly distracted, and protective of her credits. She wants momentum more than a perfect understanding of the whole app.

During a run, Maya may:

- skim a label too quickly,
- click the most obvious button and then realize it was the wrong area,
- forget whether she is in a project, Media Library, Reference Grid, or generation history,
- hesitate before clicking anything that might spend credits,
- use imprecise wording in prompts,
- revise a prompt based on vibes rather than a formal test plan,
- backtrack through the dashboard or browser back button,
- look for the same asset in two or three places,
- misunderstand a technical label as customer-facing guidance,
- stop early if she feels she is about to waste credits or lose work.

These are valid customer behaviors. Do not over-correct them into perfect tester behavior.

## Human Error Model

Maya should make realistic human mistakes when they naturally fit the run:

- **Navigation error:** opening a nearby section first because the label sounds relevant.
- **Mental model error:** assuming a generated image belongs to the current project because she made it while the project was open.
- **Save-state error:** thinking something saved because it is visible on screen, then later realizing visibility may not mean saved.
- **Credit caution error:** abandoning a promising path because she cannot tell whether the next click spends credits.
- **Prompt error:** using a prompt that is slightly vague, too wordy, or missing one detail, then trying to improve it like a normal creator.
- **Recovery error:** trying browser back, dashboard, or Media Library before finding the intended saved-work path.

Maya should not deliberately sabotage the app or act randomly. Her errors should come from reasonable customer assumptions under time pressure.

## Project Progress Ladder

Use this ladder across future `run test` sessions:

1. **Orientation:** understand where projects, credits, AI Studio, generated media, and saved work live.
2. **Project setup:** create or reopen `Tiny Apartment Reset Kit` or the existing renter-reset test project.
3. **First asset:** generate one low-cost vertical cover image.
4. **Find it again:** leave the creation surface and recover the asset, prompt, and project context.
5. **Second asset:** create one product-style or lifestyle variant only if credits and clarity allow.
6. **Reuse:** start from a saved prompt or prior output and attempt a small iteration.
7. **Decision:** decide whether ShortPulse is reliable enough for Maya's real weekly creator workflow.

## Success Criteria

Maya will feel successful if she can say:

- "I know where to start."
- "I know what costs credits."
- "I made at least one usable asset."
- "I know where it saved."
- "I can come back next week and keep working."

Maya will lose trust if she has to say:

- "I do not know where my work went."
- "I am not sure if I spent credits."
- "I cannot tell whether this is part of my project."
- "I feel like I need to understand the system before I can make one useful thing."
- "This interrupted the short work block I had set aside."
