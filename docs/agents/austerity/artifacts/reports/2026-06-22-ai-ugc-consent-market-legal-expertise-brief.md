# AI UGC, Consent, And Market Legal Expertise Brief

Date: 2026-06-22

Purpose: source-backed durable legal reference for Austerity's ShortPulse legal expert mode. This brief updates the 2026-06-21 AI UGC research with current law, FTC enforcement posture, comparator lessons, and product-specific implications.

Authority: legal-operations research artifact. It is not counsel signoff and does not override current law, provider terms, approved user decisions, or product behavior.

## Executive Posture

ShortPulse's safest legal posture is still:

- U.S.-first.
- Adult-only, 18+.
- Authenticated by default for creation.
- Private creative workspace by default.
- No public hosted UGC/community/gallery/marketplace launch until a separate UGC platform compliance gate is complete.
- No model training on private user workspace content unless explicitly approved, disclosed, technically implemented, and legally reviewed.
- Commercial use allowed only conditionally: the user must have all rights, releases, consents, and platform/legal disclosures required for their input and output use.

This posture matches ShortPulse's current product shape better than a public social/content-hosting posture.

## Current Source Highlights

### AI Output Ownership And Copyright

U.S. Copyright Office guidance remains central. The Office's AI page links the March 2023 registration guidance and the AI study. Its 2025 Part 2 report says generative AI output can be protected only where a human author determines sufficient expressive elements, including perceptible human-authored work, creative arrangement, or modification; mere prompting is not enough. Source: https://www.copyright.gov/ai/ and https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html.

ShortPulse rule:

- Never promise AI outputs are copyrightable, unique, non-infringing, or fully ownable in every jurisdiction.
- Use "as between you and ShortPulse" output-right language, subject to law, provider terms, input rights, and third-party rights.
- Commercial-use copy should be plain English and conditional, not absolute ownership language.

### FTC AI, Data, And Deception Risk

The FTC says AI companies must honor privacy and confidentiality commitments, including promises not to use customer data for training or updating models. It warns that using consumer data for undisclosed purposes, burying disclosures, or quietly changing terms can be unfair or deceptive. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/01/ai-companies-uphold-your-privacy-confidentiality-commitments.

FTC AI enforcement also continues around deceptive AI claims, refund guarantees, AI accuracy claims, and consumer harm. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/industry/technology/artificial-intelligence.

ShortPulse rule:

- Keep "no-training" separate from "product improvement." If ShortPulse does not train ShortPulse-owned foundation models on private content, say that. If logs, telemetry, support reports, safety events, or deidentified/aggregated data support product improvement, say that separately.
- Do not use "unlimited," "guaranteed," "safe for commercial use," "copyright-safe," "platform-safe," or "refund guaranteed" unless product behavior and policy support it.

### NCII, Deepfakes, And TAKE IT DOWN

The TAKE IT DOWN Act was signed on May 19, 2025. FTC enforcement of Section 3 began May 19, 2026. Covered platforms must provide a clear process for nonconsensual intimate image removal requests and remove validly reported intimate images plus known identical copies within 48 hours. The Act covers real intimate images and "digital forgeries," including AI-created or AI-altered images/videos. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/05/take-it-down-act-enforcement-starts-now-what-know-about-ftc-tida and https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/05/what-will-ftcs-enforcement-take-it-down-act-mean-you.

ShortPulse rule:

- NCII, sexual deepfakes, nudification, and sexualized likeness workflows are zero-tolerance.
- Private workspace posture lowers but does not eliminate risk.
- Public sharing, galleries, messaging, community, collaboration, or hosted output links create a separate TAKE IT DOWN/platform compliance gate.
- Even without public sharing, ShortPulse should maintain a safety@ intake, preserve records, and respond quickly to NCII reports.

### DMCA

The Copyright Office says a qualifying online service provider must publicly provide designated-agent contact information and provide the same information to the Copyright Office online DMCA Designated Agent Directory to qualify for DMCA safe harbor. Source: https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/.

ShortPulse rule:

- Do not claim registered DMCA safe harbor until the designated agent is both published and registered with the Copyright Office.
- Terms may include an intake path and a no-safe-harbor-until-registered caveat.
- Public hosted UGC makes DMCA process and repeat-infringer policy higher priority.

### Privacy, Sensitive Data, And Children

California's CCPA/CPRA grants rights to know, delete, correct, opt out of sale/share including via Global Privacy Control, limit use of sensitive personal information, and non-discrimination, subject to thresholds and exceptions. Sources: https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa and https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa/gpc.

The FTC's amended COPPA rule strengthens children's privacy protections, including separate opt-in consent for targeted advertising disclosures and limits on retention. COPPA applies to child-directed services and services with actual knowledge of under-13 users. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data and https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-coppa.

ShortPulse rule:

- Adult-only must be real product posture, policy posture, and support posture.
- Avoid collecting sensitive personal information intentionally.
- Treat voiceprints, faceprints, likenesses, and identity media as privacy-sensitive even when not formally categorized as biometric identifiers everywhere.
- Targeted advertising, retargeting pixels, sale/share, or GPC-triggered covered activity requires a fresh privacy implementation gate.

### Consent, Likeness, Voice, And Digital Replicas

Consent and publicity rights are fragmented across U.S. states and international law. Tennessee's ELVIS Act expanded state publicity rights to voice simulations effective July 1, 2024. California AB 2602 and AB 1836 address digital replicas in performer/entertainment contexts. Sources: Tennessee bill text at https://www.capitol.tn.gov/Bills/113/Bill/HB2091.pdf, California AB 2602 at https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB2602, and California AB 1836 at https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB1836.

ShortPulse rule:

- Voice cloning, text-to-voice, face/likeness image generation, motion reference, and character reference workflows must be consent-first.
- The user must warrant rights, releases, and legal permission.
- Product UI should ask for media-rights confirmation before protected creative tools and should not invite celebrity, public-figure, client, minor, or non-consenting likeness use.
- High-risk uses such as ads, endorsements, political content, impersonation, client work, talent replacement, and celebrity/public-figure simulation require strong prohibitions or counsel-approved exceptions.

### EU AI Act And DSA

The European Commission states AI Act transparency rules come into effect in August 2026. Generative AI providers must ensure AI-generated content is identifiable, and certain AI-generated content should be clearly and visibly labelled, especially deepfakes and text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai.

The Commission's 2026 Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content supports Article 50 obligations, including marking/detection for providers and deepfake/text-publication labeling for deployers. Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content.

The DSA regulates online services used in the EU, requiring transparency in content removals and appeal options for platform moderation decisions. Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act.

ShortPulse rule:

- EU active marketing/localization is not a passive default. It needs AI Act, DSA, GDPR, consumer, cookie, tax, and data-transfer review.
- If ShortPulse serves EU users or deployers, AI-output labeling/watermarking and deepfake disclosure become product requirements by 2026-08-02.
- Public UGC features trigger DSA-style notice/action, statement-of-reasons, appeal, transparency, and illegal-content controls.

### UK Online Safety

Ofcom's Online Safety Act materials state user-to-user and search services must assess and mitigate illegal harm risks, with codes covering content moderation, complaints, user access, design features, and governance. Ofcom has also updated illegal harms materials in 2026 around intimate image abuse and crisis protocols. Source: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/illegal-content-duties-under-the-online-safety-act and https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/statement-protecting-people-from-illegal-harms-online.

ShortPulse rule:

- UK availability with user-to-user/public sharing is a separate legal gate.
- Adult-only/private workspace remains safer than a public community product.
- Sexual deepfake, nudification, CSAM, self-harm, terrorism, hate, fraud, and illegal-content response controls must be mapped before UK UGC features launch.

### Subscription, Cancellation, And Refunds

The FTC's 2024 Click-to-Cancel/Negative Option Rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025, but the FTC continues to enforce unfair/deceptive subscription, AI, refund, and unauthorized-charge practices under existing authority. Sources: FTC final rule background at https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/10/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-click-cancel-rule-making-it-easier-consumers-end-recurring and Eighth Circuit opinion at https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/25/07/243137P.pdf.

ShortPulse rule:

- Even without the vacated federal rule, keep subscription disclosures plain: price, interval, renewal, cancellation path, no-proration/refund posture, credit/storage treatment, and how to cancel.
- State automatic-renewal laws and international consumer withdrawal/cooling-off rules still matter.
- Checkout and pricing copy must match the Refund Policy.

## Product Legal Doctrine For ShortPulse

1. Policy must match product behavior.
2. Consent is a workflow requirement, not just a Terms sentence.
3. Private workspace is the default launch posture.
4. Commercial use is allowed conditionally, not guaranteed.
5. "No training on private workspace content" is a promise that must be technically true.
6. Public UGC turns ShortPulse into a platform problem with DMCA, TIDA, DSA, UK OSA, moderation, appeal, and repeat-offender duties.
7. International launch is an expansion project, not an incidental byproduct of website access.
8. Counsel review is required before changing governing law/dispute terms, arbitration/class waiver, biometric posture, automatic renewal/refund posture, or EU/UK expansion.
