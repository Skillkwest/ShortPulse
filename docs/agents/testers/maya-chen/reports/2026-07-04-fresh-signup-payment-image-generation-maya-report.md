# Maya Chen Test Report: Fresh Signup, Starter Purchase, First Image

Date: 2026-07-04
Tester: Maya Chen
Surface: Production Chrome session, `https://www.shortpulse.ai`
Scenario: Create a fresh account, get into AI Studio, buy a plan, make one useful image, find it again, and download it.
Credits spent: 4

## What I Was Trying To Do

I wanted to see if I could start from basically nothing, get into ShortPulse, pay for the smallest plan that made sense, and make one image I could actually use for a short-form post.

I was thinking like a small creator who is careful with money. I did not want to start generating until I understood where I was, what credits meant, and whether my work would save somewhere.

## What Happened

The signup page was pretty clear. I created the account with email and password, then the app told me to confirm my email before signing in. That part made sense, but it did pull me out of the flow.

Once I got into AI Studio before buying anything, I saw `0 / 0` credits and a `View plans` button. I understood that I needed to buy a plan before doing real work.

The pricing page defaulted to annual billing. I noticed that and switched to monthly because I would not commit to an annual plan on a first try. I chose Starter because it was the smallest plan that still let me make images.

After payment, I landed back in AI Studio with `350 / 350` credits. That felt good. I named my project `5-minute renter reset tests`, which also helped me feel like I had a place for the work.

Then I made one image prompt:

```text
Create a calm, realistic vertical social image for a small apartment wellness creator. The image should feel warm, practical, and useful for a short-form post about a five-minute reset routine for busy renters. Natural light, tidy small kitchen or living space, realistic details, not overly glossy, no text in the image.
```

The Generate button showed `4`, so I understood this would spend 4 credits. After I clicked it, my credits changed to `346 / 350`.

The part that made me nervous was that I did not see an obvious finished result in Create. I had to go to Media to confirm the image existed. Once I went there, the image was saved, it looked nice, and it was also in the Reference Grid. I downloaded it successfully.

## How I Felt Using It

I liked the final image. It was calm, vertical, and close to what I asked for. It looked like something I could use as a background for a short post or story.

I did not like the moment after generating where I was not sure what happened. Since credits had already been spent, I wanted a very clear "your image is ready" or "saved to Media" moment. I found it, but I had to trust myself and go looking.

I also noticed that selecting the media did not show my prompt in the prompt preview area. That made me worry I would forget what wording worked.

## My Notes

- Good: Signup and email confirmation were understandable.
- Good: `View plans` was easy to find when I had no credits.
- Good: Monthly pricing was available, and Starter was clear enough to choose.
- Good: AI Studio showed the credit cost before generating.
- Good: The generated image saved to Media and downloaded correctly.
- Good: The project name showed up in Projects, so I could find it again.
- Friction: Pricing defaulted to annual, which made me slow down.
- Friction: After generation, Create did not clearly tell me the image was finished and saved.
- Friction: The prompt preview area still said the prompt preview would appear, even after selecting the generated image.
- Friction: There was another `Untitled Project` visible in Projects, which made me wonder if I accidentally made extra clutter.

## If I Were Reviewing This As A Customer

I would say the app did make the image, and the download worked, so I would not call the whole thing broken.

But I would also say: please do not make me hunt for the thing I just paid credits to create. That moment is stressful. If I spend credits, I need the app to clearly show me where the result is and reassure me it saved.

My harsh version, if I were already tired, would be: "The image was decent, but the app made me babysit the result after charging credits. I should not have to click around to prove my paid generation actually worked."
