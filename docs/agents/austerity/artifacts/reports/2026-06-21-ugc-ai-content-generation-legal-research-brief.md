# UGC AI Content Generation Legal Research Brief

Purpose: retained Austerity research brief for ShortPulse legal handling of user-generated AI content, uploads, prompts, generated outputs, media rights, voice/likeness workflows, privacy, billing-adjacent consumer rules, and international launch risk.

Status: Austerity research brief, counsel-review required before public legal claims or jurisdiction-specific launch decisions.

Research date: June 21, 2026

## Executive Conclusion

ShortPulse should be legally handled as an adult-only, private creative-workspace service that lets users upload, store, transform, and generate media through third-party AI providers. That posture is materially safer than treating ShortPulse as a social network, public publishing platform, youth product, biometric identity service, or open marketplace for AI replicas.

The safest operating model is:

- require account authentication before protected creative tools;
- keep user media private by default;
- require explicit media-rights and voice/likeness consent confirmations before upload/generation;
- prohibit illegal, deceptive, non-consensual, exploitative, and minor-sexualizing use;
- avoid public UGC distribution until takedown, moderation, DSA/OSA-style duties, and NCII/CSAM response paths are mature;
- preserve credit/billing truth without promising cash value or guaranteed commercial/legal usability of outputs;
- disclose AI/provider processing and AI-output limitations plainly;
- support privacy rights, deletion requests, and takedown/reporting channels;
- and prepare counsel review for governing law, arbitration, privacy, biometric, refund, and international launch posture.

Confidence level: high for the legal risk map and recommended product controls; partial for exact jurisdiction-specific obligations because final applicability depends on launch geography, audience, traffic, public-sharing features, age posture, entity details, marketing stack, and counsel review.

## Product Classification For Legal Purposes

ShortPulse currently fits best as a private SaaS creative workspace with AI-provider integrations, not as a public UGC platform.

That distinction matters:

- Private workspace: primary duties are contract, privacy, IP, billing, provider-terms flowdown, security, consent, and abuse prevention.
- Hosting/public sharing: adds notice-and-action, repeat-infringer, illegal-content reporting, public moderation, DSA/OSA-style obligations, takedown appeals, transparency, and stronger content governance.
- Youth service: adds COPPA, age-assurance, child-safety, teen privacy, platform design, and jurisdictional youth-code risk.
- Voice/likeness/face service: adds consent, right of publicity, biometric/sensitive data, impersonation, NCII, fraud, and deepfake disclosure risk.
- Advertising/content-marketing tool: adds FTC/ASA/UCPD truth-in-advertising, endorsement, synthetic influencer, platform disclosure, and AI-labeling risk.

Recommendation: keep ShortPulse's launch posture as adult-only, authenticated, private-by-default, and non-publication-first until a separate public-sharing legal gate is passed.

## U.S. Legal Framework

### Copyright And AI Output

Current source-backed rule: U.S. copyright protection requires human authorship. The Copyright Office treats fully machine-generated expressive output as not registrable by itself, while human selection, arrangement, editing, or other creative contribution may support copyright in the human-authored parts.

ShortPulse implication:

- Terms should say users may use outputs subject to law, input rights, provider terms, and third-party rights.
- Terms should not promise outputs are copyrightable, exclusive, unique, non-infringing, or protectable.
- UI/help text should encourage users to preserve human edits and project records if they plan to claim copyright.
- ShortPulse should not market "copyright-free" or "you fully own every output" without careful caveats.

### Copyright Infringement And DMCA

If ShortPulse stores user-uploaded or user-generated content, DMCA safe-harbor hygiene is high ROI even before public sharing.

Required posture before public launch:

- designate a DMCA/copyright agent with the U.S. Copyright Office if seeking safe harbor;
- publish copyright contact information on the site;
- include takedown/counter-notice procedure and repeat-infringer policy;
- create an internal process for prompt removal/disablement and evidence preservation;
- do not treat Section 230 as copyright protection because Section 230 does not cover IP claims.

### Section 230

Section 230 can protect interactive computer services from being treated as publisher/speaker of third-party content, but it has major limits.

ShortPulse implication:

- It may help for defamation-style claims over user-provided content, especially if ShortPulse does not materially develop the harmful content.
- It does not cover federal criminal law, IP claims, electronic privacy claims, or content ShortPulse itself materially creates/develops.
- AI generation complicates the analysis because ShortPulse may be more than a passive host when it helps generate or transform output.

Recommendation: treat Section 230 as a secondary defense, not as a product-design strategy.

### FTC, Deception, Impersonation, Advertising, And AI Claims

FTC posture is clear: AI does not create an exemption from unfair/deceptive-practices law. AI claims, synthetic endorsements, impersonation, hidden ads, fake reviews, fake influencer/persona content, and misleading performance claims are all risk areas.

ShortPulse implication:

- Do not overstate what ShortPulse can generate, predict, optimize, or guarantee.
- Require users to comply with ad, endorsement, platform, and consumer-protection rules.
- Prohibit impersonation of government, businesses, officials, brands, and individuals.
- Include acceptable-use rules against fake testimonials, fake endorsements, scam ads, deceptive political/public-interest content, and synthetic-person deception.
- Add user-facing "disclose AI where required" language for realistic media and ads.

### AI Voice Calls, Political Content, And Election Deepfakes

The FCC has treated AI-generated voices in robocalls as "artificial or prerecorded voice" technology under the TCPA, requiring prior express consent absent an emergency purpose or exemption. The FEC declined to open a dedicated AI campaign-ad rulemaking in 2024, but adopted interpretive guidance that existing fraudulent-misrepresentation rules can apply to AI campaign ads. State election deepfake laws are moving quickly and vary widely.

ShortPulse implication:

- Prohibit using ShortPulse voice/audio outputs for robocalls, robotexts, scams, voter suppression, or political impersonation without all required consent and disclosures.
- Treat political, election, public-official, government, and civic-information content as high risk.
- Do not market ShortPulse for political deepfakes, voter persuasion through deception, or synthetic candidate/public-official impersonation.
- Add AUP language requiring compliance with election, campaign, telemarketing, TCPA, platform, and AI-disclosure rules.

### Child Safety, CSAM, And COPPA

COPPA applies to services directed to children under 13 or with actual knowledge of under-13 users. U.S. providers also face mandatory CyberTipline reporting duties when they become aware of suspected CSAM, online enticement, or child sex trafficking on their services.

The Take It Down Act adds U.S. federal requirements around nonconsensual intimate imagery, including AI-created digital forgeries, and platform notice/removal expectations.

ShortPulse implication:

- Keep public posture 18+ only unless/until youth compliance is deliberately designed.
- Prohibit any sexual, exploitative, grooming, or abusive content involving minors, including AI-generated or altered content.
- Create an internal CSAM/NCMEC escalation SOP before broad launch.
- Create an NCII/TIDA takedown intake and 48-hour response target before public hosting/sharing.
- Do not allow adult sexual generation or nudification tools without age assurance, consent tooling, content moderation, and counsel review.

### Right Of Publicity, Voice, Likeness, Digital Replicas

U.S. law is fragmented. States increasingly protect voice, image, likeness, and digital replicas; Tennessee's ELVIS Act and California performer/deceased-person digital-replica laws show the direction. The Copyright Office also recommended a federal digital-replica law, though federal law remains evolving.

ShortPulse implication:

- Voice cloning must require affirmative consent/legal right.
- Face/likeness/persona generation must require user representations and be prohibited for deception, harassment, sexualization, exploitation, impersonation, or commercial misuse.
- Celebrity/public figure and private-person workflows should be treated as high risk unless clearly licensed or parodic/newsworthy with counsel-reviewed limits.
- Keep audit records for voice-clone consent and ownership where possible.

### Privacy, Biometrics, And Sensitive Data

ShortPulse processes prompts, uploads, generated media, voice samples, face/likeness material, billing records, telemetry, and support reports. Some jurisdictions treat biometric identifiers, voiceprints, face geometry, precise identity data, minors' data, health, financial, and other categories as sensitive.

ShortPulse implication:

- Publish a privacy policy with notice at collection, categories, purposes, providers, retention, rights, sale/share posture, sensitive data, and contact.
- If face/voice processing ever extracts biometric templates or uniquely identifies people, require biometric-specific counsel review.
- Treat Illinois BIPA, Texas CUBI, Washington biometric law, CCPA sensitive data, Colorado/Virginia sensitive-data consent, GDPR Article 9, UK GDPR, Australia Privacy Act, and Canada privacy principles as high-risk triggers.
- Avoid claiming "we do not process biometric data" unless confirmed technically.
- Avoid targeted advertising/sale/share until opt-outs and GPC-like signals are handled.

### Subscriptions, Negative Options, And Cancellation

Recurring subscriptions remain a consumer-law risk even though the FTC's 2024 Click-to-Cancel rule has had a contested/vacated status after litigation. The practical rule remains: disclose subscription terms clearly, get affirmative consent, make cancellation easy, preserve billing evidence, and check state laws such as California's automatic-renewal requirements.

ShortPulse implication:

- Keep cancellation at least as easy as sign-up where feasible.
- Clearly disclose recurring price, billing interval, renewal, credits, storage add-ons, cancellation timing, downgrade timing, and no-refund/default refund posture before purchase.
- Do not rely on a single federal rule status; state automatic-renewal laws still matter.
- Preserve Stripe/customer-portal behavior proof before public billing-policy claims.

## International Legal Framework

### European Union

EU AI Act:

- Article 50 creates transparency obligations for certain AI-generated/synthetic/deepfake content.
- Providers/deployers of AI systems may need to mark synthetic outputs or disclose deepfakes, depending on role and feature.
- General-purpose model obligations mostly sit with model providers, but ShortPulse can still be a deployer/provider for its app layer.

Digital Services Act:

- If ShortPulse hosts user content for EU users, especially public content, it may need notice-and-action mechanisms, terms enforcement transparency, complaint handling, and illegal-content processes.
- Very large platform duties likely do not apply at launch, but hosting obligations can apply at much smaller scale.

GDPR:

- Personal data in prompts/uploads/generation metadata requires a lawful basis, transparency, data subject rights, processor/controller analysis, security, retention, international transfers, and DPIA-style risk review where processing is high risk.
- Special category data and biometric identification require heightened analysis.

Consumer law:

- EU consumers generally have 14-day withdrawal rights for distance contracts unless a valid digital-content/service exception is implemented.
- Subscription, cancellation, price, refund, and credit terms need EU-specific review before EU consumer launch.

Recommendation: do not market EU availability until AI transparency, privacy, consumer withdrawal, and DSA hosting posture are reviewed.

### United Kingdom

The UK Online Safety Act regulates user-to-user services, search services, and services publishing pornographic content. Ofcom has indicated not all AI chatbots are regulated, but user-to-user/public hosting and pornographic capability can trigger duties. UK data protection law applies to AI processing of personal data, and UK advertising regulators expect AI use to be transparent where material and non-obvious.

ShortPulse implication:

- Private generation is lower risk than public user-to-user sharing.
- Any public sharing, pornographic/adult output, likely-child access, or intimate-image content requires UK OSA review.
- UK privacy and AI fairness/transparency expectations should be folded into privacy and acceptable-use posture.

### Canada

Canada does not have a fully enacted comprehensive federal AI statute equivalent to the EU AI Act as of this research, but privacy regulators have issued generative AI principles emphasizing legal authority, consent where required, limits, transparency, accuracy, safeguards, accountability, and rights.

ShortPulse implication:

- Treat Canadian launch as privacy-first and consent/notice-heavy.
- Do not rely on lack of a comprehensive AI statute as absence of obligations.

### Australia

Australia treats biometric information as sensitive information under the Privacy Act where covered. The eSafety Commissioner actively addresses image-based abuse, including AI deepfake abuse.

ShortPulse implication:

- Voice/face workflows need consent-sensitive handling.
- Public sharing or abuse-report intake should support image-based abuse reporting and fast removal review.

### China

Mainland China has specific generative AI and deep synthesis rules for public-facing services, including content controls, labeling, data/privacy duties, and potentially filings/security assessments.

ShortPulse implication:

- Do not launch public-facing generative AI service into mainland China without dedicated China counsel, localization, content governance, algorithm/deep-synthesis filing analysis, labeling controls, and provider/data-transfer review.
- Practical default: geofence or exclude China until a specific China launch plan exists.

## Provider And Platform Obligations

Provider terms flow down to ShortPulse and users.

OpenAI:

- Users may own output as against OpenAI to the extent permitted by law, but OpenAI usage policies prohibit CSAM, minor exploitation, nonconsensual sexual content, deception, and other harmful uses.

ElevenLabs:

- Voice replication without consent/legal right, harmful use, unauthorized sexualization, and deception about AI generation are prohibited.

Fal:

- Terms and trust/safety policies require lawful/responsible use and prohibit unlawful content such as CSAM and NCII.

Social platforms:

- YouTube requires disclosure of realistic AI-generated or meaningfully altered content.
- TikTok requires labels for realistic AI-generated images/audio/video.
- Meta labels AI-generated/manipulated media and has evolving disclosure/labeling policies.

ShortPulse implication:

- Terms must require users to comply with provider and destination-platform policies.
- Product should provide export reminders/disclosure guidance for realistic AI media.
- ShortPulse should not guarantee that outputs will be accepted by social/ad platforms.

## Policy Requirements

Terms of Service should include:

- eligibility and authority to bind business/client;
- user account responsibility;
- user content license to provide service;
- rights/consent warranties for uploads, prompts, media, likeness, voice, minors, and client material;
- AI output ownership/use caveat;
- no warranty of uniqueness, copyrightability, legality, platform acceptance, or commercial fitness;
- provider terms flowdown;
- acceptable-use prohibitions;
- copyright/takedown process;
- suspension/termination and repeat-infringer posture;
- liability, indemnity, dispute, governing law, and contact.

Privacy Policy should include:

- categories collected;
- purposes;
- AI provider processing;
- billing/payment processor handling;
- telemetry and local/session storage;
- sensitive/biometric caveat;
- retention;
- sharing/provider categories;
- state privacy rights;
- international transfers;
- children/minors;
- sale/share/targeted advertising posture;
- contact and rights process.

Refund Policy should include:

- subscriptions renew and cancel through billing controls/Stripe;
- credit packs generally final;
- credits no cash value;
- failures release/restore credits when billing records support it;
- no refund for aesthetic dissatisfaction, prompt mistakes, platform rejection, abandoned flows, or used credits;
- storage add-ons recurring;
- taxes/fees/currency;
- chargebacks;
- legal exceptions and support review path.

Acceptable Use should either be in Terms or a standalone policy:

- no illegal content;
- no CSAM/minor sexualization;
- no NCII, sexual deepfakes, nudification, sexualized likenesses, or intimate imagery without consent;
- no unauthorized voice/likeness cloning;
- no impersonation/deception/fraud/scams;
- no rights infringement;
- no malware/security abuse;
- no hate/harassment/threats;
- no political/public-interest deception without required disclosures;
- no robocalls, robotexts, voter suppression, synthetic campaign deception, or public-official impersonation without all required consent, authority, and disclosure;
- no regulated professional advice as final advice without qualified professional review;
- no provider/platform policy evasion.

## Product Design Recommendations

- Add a persistent "rights and disclosure" help path near uploads/generation, not just Terms.
- Keep `Confirm media rights` as an explicit legal gate and version it.
- Add voice-specific consent copy before custom voice clone/use.
- Add output export reminder: "Realistic AI-generated or altered content may require disclosure on platforms like YouTube, TikTok, Meta, or under local law."
- Add report links for copyright, privacy, impersonation/likeness, NCII, safety/abuse, and billing.
- Preserve server-side records for consent acceptance, voice ownership, generation IDs, billing events, and takedown handling.
- Avoid public gallery/community features until moderation and takedown machinery exists.
- Avoid "celebrity voice," "deepfake anyone," "AI influencer pretending to be real," "nudify," or "undetectable AI" marketing language.
- Avoid using user content to train ShortPulse-owned models unless opt-in consent, privacy disclosures, deletion controls, and provider/data rights are redesigned.

## Austerity Standing Legal Rules From This Research

- Treat AI output rights as conditional, not absolute.
- Treat voice, face, likeness, and identity workflows as consent-first and high risk.
- Treat minors and intimate imagery as zero-tolerance legal safety categories.
- Treat privacy disclosures as product-specific: prompts/uploads/outputs/provider processing must be named.
- Treat Section 230 as helpful but not enough, especially for AI-assisted creation, IP, federal crimes, and platform duties.
- Treat public sharing as a separate launch gate from private generation.
- Treat international launch as a compliance expansion, not a default side effect of having a website.
